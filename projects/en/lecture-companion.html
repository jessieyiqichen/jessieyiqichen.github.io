Problem

Plenty of questions, zero asked.

In an online class, questions come up all the time, but asking means pausing, putting the question into words and switching windows, and by the time you finish typing, the lecture has moved on to the next page.

  • Questions come up often, yet almost none get asked: the gap is the effort one question costs.
  • A general-purpose chatbot needs you to describe the problem clearly first, the most expensive action in class.
  • A text transcript alone isn't enough: the board writing, formulas and diagrams are all in the picture.

"The bottleneck is input friction, not model capability."

— This judgment set all of the interaction design that followed
Iteration 1 · Zero-typing marks

Make asking a single click.

If input is the bottleneck, remove the input: click whichever sentence confuses you, and the same click hands a screenshot to a multimodal model to restore the board writing and formulas.

ValidationAcross 9 class sessions: 63 marks, 59 with zero typing. Bring the cost of asking down to one click, and asking happens.

Iteration 2 · Multimodal retrieval

Answers cite a slide page number, and the page number cannot be made up.

Slide formulas and diagrams existed only in the page images. The second iteration fuses each page image and its text into a single vector; page numbers come from the retrieval layer, never the model, so they cannot be made up.

How it shipped

Three rollout stages, one layer changed per stage.

Indexing, retrieval and citation display shipped in three stages, fully merged in two days, zero rollbacks.

Why I'm strict about this

Being traceable matters more than looking smart.

I can trust an answer that carries a page number, and click back to check it.

North Star

The North Star sits after class, not in class.

The product delivers its value after class, so the North Star is "marks reviewed". The review flow has been entered 108 times; early on, more than 60% of entries fell in exam and assignment weeks.

Unit Economics

Keep the books first, then cut cost. To cut cost, first prove quality holds.

One two-hour class costs about ¥5.5, and ASR accounts for more than 80% of that, so cost cutting focuses only on ASR; the LLM side is about ¥1 per class.

  • Shadow run. The old and new ASR tiers ran side by side in real classes: zero timestamp residual, sentence-level similarity of 0.98 or higher.
  • Result. Switching to the same vendor's tier at one quarter of the unit price cut the monthly cost from ¥90 to ¥25 (measured over 8 classes, 11.25 hours).
  • The order cannot be reversed. Ledger first, then the quality check, and only then the switch.
