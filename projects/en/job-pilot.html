JobPilot

Tools on the market help you apply more and faster; this one wants to understand you as a person first.

A job-search strategist that stays with you from finding roles to getting an offer: it knows how you think, how you talk, and where you get stuck. The differentiation is not the number of features but the layer of modeling. Others stop at the behavior layer; it plugs in a cognitive model and language samples. Problem

The job search is blocked by people, not information.

When I was job hunting myself, I found that what really held me back was not a lack of job information:

  • I picked out 37 high-score jobs and tailored 19 résumés, and did not send out a single application.
  • I had to edit the AI-generated opening messages every time, because they did not sound like me.
  • With two offers in front of me, I did not know what criteria to choose by.

Mass-application tools and template tools cannot solve these. They stop at the behavior layer: they only look at what you did, not at why you did it.

“Its diagnosis of my 0 applications was not ‘keep going, apply faster’ but ‘you are holding the things you care about to too high a standard; let yourself send an imperfect version first.’ Keyword matching cannot say something like that.”

— The first genuinely useful diagnosis the strategist gave me
Insight

Moving from the behavior layer to the cognitive layer.

This is the fundamental difference between it and the tools on the market, and the one thing the whole project sets out to prove:

Catching the contradictions between “what you say” and “what you do”

Declared and inferred are kept clearly apart. Your declared preferences (city, deal-breakers) are hard rules the strategist must respect; inferred cognition is the understanding layer, which helps it adjust how it talks and see where you are stuck. Where the two clash is often where the most valuable insight lies.

Decisions

Six trade-offs, each with the why thought through.

  • No auto-apply, no scrapers. Applying is irreversible, and platforms have anti-scraping boundaries. The positioning is “we generate, you send”: restraint in exchange for trust.
  • From the behavior layer to the cognitive layer. It plugs in a cognitive model (how you think) and language samples (how you talk); that is the moat. The cost is being bound deeply to one user.
  • Plans and follow-ups are computed by rules, without burning LLM calls. If “which ones to apply to this week” can be computed, it does not call the API. That saves money, and every suggestion points back to data, so it holds up to “based on what?”
  • Two-stage scoring. A free heuristic first does a rough pass over all jobs, and only the top N get a precise evaluation through the API, so the cost of a full run stays under control.
  • Résumé tailoring patches the original docx instead of regenerating it. difflib patches the changes back into the original file, keeping fonts and layout; a skills whitelist stops the AI from writing in abilities I do not have.
  • No rush to go multi-user. The strongest personalization comes precisely from being bound deeply to one person; generalizing too early would dilute it into one more ordinary tool. First prove it is useful to one person.

Engineering baselinePipeline stages are isolated from one another, so one failing channel does not affect the others; without an API key it falls back to heuristics, and the basic functions keep working. pipeline-all runs the whole flow in one command.

Honest Limits

It works, and I have thought through where the next step gets hard.

The real problem

Not multi-user, but cold start.

The data layer has long been isolated per user, so multi-user is not hard to engineer. The hard part is how a user it has never met can quickly get this depth of understanding. Right now the strongest personalization comes precisely from being deeply bound to me alone; generalizing too early would dilute it into an ordinary tool.

My plan

Progressive depth.

A new user fills in only a 5-minute questionnaire and gives a few messages they wrote themselves, and then it understands them better the more they use it: every conversation collects cognitive signals, and every edit to a message makes the system more like them. The aim is not to be accurate from the start, but to be like someone who has been with you for three months.

“What this project sets out to prove is not how many features I can write, but that I have thought through the why of every decision.”

— Closing line of the project case study
