Problem

The job search is blocked by people, not information.

What held me back was not a lack of job information; mass-application and template tools only look at what you did, not why:

  • I picked out 37 high-score jobs, tailored 19 résumés, and sent out not a single application.
  • I had to edit the AI’s opening messages every time, because they did not sound like me.
  • With two offers in front of me, I did not know what criteria to choose by.

“Its diagnosis of my 0 applications was not ‘keep going, apply faster’ but ‘you are holding the things you care about to too high a standard; let yourself send an imperfect version first.’ Keyword matching cannot say something like that.”

— The first genuinely useful diagnosis the strategist gave me
Insight

Moving from the behavior layer to the cognitive layer.

Your declared preferences are hard rules; inferred cognition only adjusts how it talks, and where the two clash is often the most valuable.

Decisions

Six trade-offs, each with the why thought through.

  • No auto-apply, no scrapers. Applying is irreversible, so it is “we generate, you send”: restraint in exchange for trust.
  • From the behavior layer to the cognitive layer. That is the moat, and the cost is being bound deeply to one user.
  • Plans and follow-ups are computed by rules, without burning LLM calls. If it can be computed, it does not call the API, and every suggestion points back to data.
  • Two-stage scoring. A free heuristic does a rough pass over all jobs, and only the top N get a precise API evaluation.
  • Résumé tailoring patches the original docx instead of regenerating it. The layout is kept, and a skills whitelist stops the AI from inventing abilities.
  • No rush to go multi-user. Generalizing too early would dilute it into an ordinary tool, so first prove it is useful to one person.

Engineering baselinePipeline stages are isolated from one another, and without an API key it falls back to heuristics; pipeline-all runs the whole flow in one command.

Honest Limits

It works, and I have thought through where the next step gets hard.

The real problem

Not multi-user, but cold start.

The data layer is already isolated per user; the hard part is how a user it has never met can quickly get this depth of understanding.

My plan

Progressive depth.

A new user fills in a 5-minute questionnaire and a few messages, then it learns with use, like someone who has been with you for three months.

“What this project sets out to prove is not how many features I can write, but that I have thought through the why of every decision.”

— Closing line of the project case study
