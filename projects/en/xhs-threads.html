Problem

The bookmarks keep coming; the thoughts get lost.

Every time you tap save, there is a thought: my project could use this; this post contradicts last week’s. But the folder stores only the content, and months later it is a pile of links you cannot remember why you saved.

  • Saving takes seconds and happens daily; organizing means sitting down to do it, so it basically never happens.
  • Expecting users to build an organizing habit pushes the problem back onto them; the right question is: can organizing need no user involvement?
  • What is valuable is “why you saved it at the time,” not content categories, and once lost it cannot be recovered.
Differentiation

Competitors wait for the user to start; I let the system act on its own.

A survey of four competitor types found that existing tools treat organizing as content categorization, and most need the user to start it; none was zero-label and grew with the bookmarks. I wrote the differentiation up as 8 product judgments.

Evaluation

What the AI writes for users has to pass a quality check first.

Text the AI writes goes straight to users, in volume, and cannot be checked by hand item by item; a failing item is rewritten, a failed rewrite goes to a human, and nothing is silently let through.

Pre-launch acceptance

7 bad samples, all caught.

Before launch I built 7 typical bad samples (fabrication, empty talk, wrong thread) and launched only after review caught every one.

Key mechanism

The reviewer model must be independent.

A model grading itself is the same as no grading, so review runs on a different model, three reviews take the majority, and the criteria are written down.

Evaluation literacy

I even measured the error of the ruler itself.

Threading accuracy measured once was 30/36 (83%), against 31% for the strongest trivial baseline; rerunning the same criteria four times gave 30/28/28/31, so any “improvement” below the ±3 noise does not count.

Unit Economics

15 calls per note, 90% on the light tier.

One note takes 15 model calls on average (from the ledger: 622 calls, 41 notes). All-flagship costs too much and all-light is unstable, so classification goes to the light tier and writing to the flagship tier.

  • Every call is logged. Purpose, tier and tokens are all recorded, and the cost report reproduces with one command.
  • Cost per item. About 0.05–0.10 yuan; early ledger entries mixed in evaluation calls, and after fixing the bookkeeping I am re-measuring.
  • A quality backstop. Saving money cannot come from quietly lowering quality, so failing output is still sent back for a rewrite.
