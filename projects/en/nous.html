Problem

Replying to a message is a decision, not text generation.

Behind every message is a string of small decisions: reply or not, when to reply, say yes or not. A platform assistant can write a polite reply, but it does not know how you would decide:

  • It does not know whether this message is worth replying to for you: it has no model of your attention.
  • It does not know whether you will actually do what you agree to: it has no record of how you follow through.
  • It lives on a single platform, while your messages are spread across WeChat, DingTalk and Feishu.

“Tone can be imitated; cognitive structure cannot.”

— Platform assistants answer “how to reply politely”; Nous answers “how you would reply”
Product

One brain, three entry points, living inside the chat apps you already use.

Not a new app: a Feishu bot catches messages on your phone, a Mac floating assistant floats above WeChat and DingTalk, and all three clients share the same cognitive model.

Suggestions float above WeChat; your focus never leaves the conversation.

Notification banners detect new messages automatically, or you press ⌘C twice to summon it; click “Fill into input box” and the draft goes straight into the chat box. When it stays out of the way matters as much as when it shows up.

Feishu bot · official API

The mobile entry point, routed around a 3-second timeout.

Webhooks hit a 3-second timeout on the cross-border link, so I switched to long-connection mode: the client connects to Feishu itself. Official APIs throughout, zero risk of account bans.

Signature Feature

“You’ll say yes, but you can’t follow through.”

The cognitive model records 20 contradictions between “what you say” and “what you do.” When it predicts that your commitment will fall through, it says so plainly, and scales the commitment in the draft down to what you will really do.

“You’ll most likely say yes, but by your habits you won’t take this to heart, and odds are you’ll put it off until Sunday night. The draft relaxes the deadline to Monday, leaving yourself a buffer.”

— Real output: the execution quality check for a manager assigning work over the weekend

Platform assistants cannot do this: it needs long-term modeling of one person’s behavior, and “calling the user out” runs against the product logic of pleasing the user.

Learning Loop

Teach it one rule, and it knows that rule.

The style card learns how you type from your real writing; the correction flywheel feeds every rewrite you make to a draft into the next generation as an example. Teach it one rule, “a refusal should be direct and offer an alternative,” ask again in the same scenario, and the draft uses it immediately.

Research Foundation

Under the product: model first, then validate, and fix what is wrong.

An AI interviewer uses 15–25 rounds of conversation to map nine dimensions, then tests the model by predicting behavior in new scenarios: the hit rate is an external standard, not a matter of how it feels.

  • A validation loop. Behavior prediction accuracy went from 49% on the first test to 71% through iteration (random baseline 25%), and model understanding accuracy reached 99%.
  • Contradictions are the most honest data. “What you say” and “what you do” are recorded separately: 20 pieces of blind-spot evidence found so far.
  • Passive collection. In the background it extracts cognitive signals from everyday AI sessions: 101 so far.

The method works on strangersThe first stranger participant’s single 23-round interview produced a nine-dimension profile, 8 contradictions and 52 signals.
