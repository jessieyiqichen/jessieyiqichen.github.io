MusiClaw

A performance market, turned into a data product that updates itself every day.

Every day it collects public ticketing data on Shanghai musicals and produces weekly reports, a dashboard and an AI morning brief for a Xiaohongshu data account in real operation.

Problem

Theatre fans talk about everything except data.

  • Anyone can look at a seat map, but no one saves it as a time series that can be analyzed.
  • "Which cast sells tickets" and "which performances sell well" rest entirely on word of mouth in the fan community.
  • Performance content on social platforms runs on emotion and taking sides, and data-backed discussion is almost nonexistent, which is exactly the opening for content positioning.

"Account persona: a theatre fan who brings data. Someone new to musicals who can write code."

— Content positioning: the data skill itself is the differentiation

AI Boundaries

The AI handles data to draft; the publish button is always pressed by a person.

In this project the AI acts as a publicity specialist, with eight responsibilities written into a document; what it does not do is written down too.

What the AI does

Everything from data to draft.

It finds anomalies in each day's data, proposes topics and drafts content, checks the post before publishing, and reads the data for a review afterward.

What the AI doesn't do

It doesn't touch publishing or style.

It doesn't click publish automatically, make decisions for a person, "optimize" my style on its own, or write the meme copy it can't write well.

"What this project taught me most is boundary design: only when you know where to make the AI stop can you safely put it into a real workflow."

— A takeaway after building this pipeline
Content Discipline

The credibility of data content comes from discipline.

  • Every chart is labeled with its collection time and completeness. A seat map is a snapshot, not real time, so leaving that out misleads.
  • Three fixed checks before publishing. Throttling-risk words, AI tone, marketing-account tone: if a post fails, it doesn't go out.
  • A fixed review 24 hours after publishing. Read the data and archive the assets to inform the next topic.
  • Every past post is archived. Each one's topic rationale, data source and performance can be looked up again.
