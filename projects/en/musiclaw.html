MusiClaw

A performance market, turned into a data product that updates itself every day.

Information in Shanghai's musical-theatre market is highly opaque: ticket sales, the effect of the cast, venue and time-slot effects. Fans go by feel; the industry goes by experience. Every day MusiClaw automatically collects public seat-map snapshots for 86 shows, combines them with cast schedule data to analyze sell-through rates, and produces weekly reports, a data dashboard and an AI morning brief, supporting a Xiaohongshu data account in real operation. From data collection to content publishing, it is one complete closed loop.

Problem

Theatre fans talk about everything except data.

  • Ticketing data is actually public; anyone can look at a seat map. But no one saves it and turns it into a time series that can be analyzed.
  • "Which cast sells tickets" and "which performances sell well" rest entirely on word of mouth inside the fan community, with no data to speak for them.
  • Performance content on social platforms runs on emotion and taking sides; data-backed discussion is almost nonexistent. That is an information gap, and also an opening for content positioning.

"Account persona: a theatre fan who brings data. Someone new to musicals who can write code."

— Content positioning: the data skill itself is the differentiation

AI Boundaries

The AI handles data to draft; the publish button is always pressed by a person.

In this project the AI acts as a publicity specialist, and its eight responsibilities are written into a document: data insight, topic selection, content review, data verification, asset preparation, posting assistance, comment management and post-publish review. Also written into the document: what it does not do.

What the AI does

Everything from data to draft.

It finds anomalies in each day's data, proposes topics and drafts content; before publishing it checks for throttling-risk words and AI tone; 24 hours after publishing it reads the data for a review.

What the AI doesn't do

It doesn't touch publishing or style.

It doesn't click publish automatically, doesn't make publishing decisions for a person, doesn't "optimize" my writing style on its own, and doesn't write the meme-style copy it can't write well.

"What this project taught me most is boundary design: only when you know where to make the AI stop can you safely put it into a real workflow."

— A takeaway after building this pipeline
Content Discipline

The credibility of data content comes from discipline.

  • Every chart is labeled with its collection time and completeness. A seat map is a snapshot, not real time; leaving that unlabeled misleads readers.
  • Three fixed checks before publishing. Throttling-risk words, AI tone, marketing-account tone. If a post doesn't pass, it doesn't go out.
  • A fixed review 24 hours after publishing. Read the post's data and archive the assets; review data feeds back into topic judgment.
  • Every past post is archived. For each one, the reason for the topic, the data source and the performance can all be looked up again.
